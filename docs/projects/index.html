    Research

    As a core research member, I contribute to an NSFC General Program project (2025–2028; total funding RMB 480,000) and a Beijing Natural Science Foundation–Haidian Joint Fund project (2023–2026; RMB 200,000), focusing on exoskeleton-based musculoskeletal protection and orthopedic rehabilitation. Earlier, I led or participated in two National Undergraduate Innovation Training projects (RMB 10,000 and 20,000) and led a BJUT Xinghuo Key Project (RMB 3,000). These experiences connect mechanical design, prototyping, sensing, control and system validation toward bringing exoskeletons from the laboratory into everyday life.

    RESEARCH AREA / 01

    Mechatronic Design of Cable-Driven Exoskeletons

    I investigate the co-design of actuation mechanisms, transmissions and wearable interfaces to bring exoskeletons from laboratory prototypes toward everyday use. Switchable actuation, reconfigurable systems and variable-stiffness mechanisms support different assistance tasks and operating settings.

    • Develop a cable-driven actuator that switches between torque and tension output.
    • Build a hip exoskeleton with interchangeable bench and backpack configurations; switching averaged 30.1 ± 16.3 s across 30 trials with three participants.
    • Investigate coupled movable pulley rehabilitation mechanisms and crossed four-bar variable-stiffness actuation.

    RESEARCH AREA / 02

    Intelligent Sensing and Motion Planning for Exoskeletons

    Assistance in real-world settings requires an exoskeleton to recognize the user’s gait state and changing movement needs. My work connects continuous gait-state estimation and multimodal parameter awareness with assistive trajectory planning.

    • Estimate gait phase in real time using implicit modeling of human locomotion, with a reported steady-state phase RMSE of 2.729%.
    • Review assistive trajectory-planning strategies and their progression from laboratory-optimized gait toward environmentally adaptive locomotion.

    RESEARCH AREA / 03

    Safe and Compliant Human–Robot Interaction

    For robots in direct physical contact with people, I study safe admittance boundaries and transition control. These methods support motion within defined interaction regions and compliant transitions between assistance directions.

    • Define safe admittance boundaries for rehabilitation robots using a space-classification model.
    • Investigate compliant transitions between plantarflexion and dorsiflexion assistance in a bidirectional cable-driven ankle exoskeleton.
